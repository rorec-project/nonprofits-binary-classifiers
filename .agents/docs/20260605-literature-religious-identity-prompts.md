## 2. Why These Matter

- **Best survey wording sources**: Pew, GSS, WVS/EVS, NCS, ARDA Measurement Wizard. These provide reusable question stems, response categories, and translation/comparability warnings.
- **Best organizational coding sources**: Sider & Unruh; Smith & Sosin; Bielefeld & Cleveland; Ebaugh/Chafetz/Pipes; NCS. These separate identity, authority/auspice, staff/governance, support, and program content.
- **Best computational sources**: Ma; Fyall/Gugerty/Moore; religious hate taxonomy; Twitter religious landscape; name-inference; religiolect corpora; GivingTuesday model. These show how keyword dictionaries, supervised classifiers, expert annotation, and name/text features behave.

## 4. Synthesis: What Best Distinguishes the Constructs

1. **Religious identity / affiliation** is best captured by self-identification wording (“present religion, if any”) or explicit organizational self-presentation (name, mission, “about,” history, statement of faith). Do not infer identity solely from values words like service, compassion, or mission.
2. **Denomination / tradition** requires named denominations, congregations, official associations, or follow-up probes. Pew and NCS both show that “Christian,” “Baptist,” or “church” may be too coarse.
3. **Practice / religiosity** is distinct from affiliation: attendance, prayer, scripture reading, religious services, worship, sacraments, proselytizing, chaplaincy, and devotional programming indicate religious activity, but not necessarily organizational auspice.
4. **Mission / faith-based organizational identity** is strongest when mission/history/name connects the organization to a religious or spiritual tradition, and stronger still when coupled with religious activities or governance/staff requirements.
5. **Auspice / control** needs evidence of formal affiliation, sponsorship, board appointment, religious order/diocese/congregation/federation control, or funding/volunteer flows from a faith community.
6. **Faith-inspired ambiguity** arises when organizations use religious roots or broad spirituality but provide secular services without explicit religious content. Sider & Unruh’s “faith-background” and “faith-affiliated” categories are useful middle labels.

## 5. Practical Recommendations for LLM Prompts, Weak Supervision, and Human Annotation

### LLM prompt wording

Use a multi-part prompt rather than a single binary question:

> Classify whether the organization expresses a religious or spiritual identity or mission. Use only evidence in the name, mission, program descriptions, history, affiliation, governance, staff/volunteer requirements, religious practices, or explicit religious language. Separate: (A) religious identity/affiliation, (B) religious activity/program content, (C) historical religious roots only, and (D) ambiguous spiritual language. Do not count generic service words (“mission,” “ministry,” “serve,” “community”) unless tied to explicit religious tradition, practice, scripture, worship, denomination, congregation, or faith-based affiliation. Return label, confidence, evidence spans, and caveats.

### Weak-supervision labeling functions

- **High precision positive**: named denomination/tradition + organizational terms: `church`, `synagogue`, `mosque`, `temple`, `Catholic Charities`, `Lutheran`, `Episcopal`, `Islamic`, `Jewish`, `Baptist`, `Presbyterian`, `United Methodist`, `Sisters of`, `diocese`, `congregation`, `ministry` with religious co-term.
- **Religious activity positive**: `worship`, `prayer`, `scripture`, `Bible study`, `Gospel`, `Torah`, `Quran`, `chaplain`, `pastoral care`, `missionary`, `evangelism`, `discipleship`, `religious education`, `sacrament`, `zakat`, `halal`, `kosher`.
- **Ambiguous / review**: `faith`, `spiritual`, `sacred`, `ministry`, `mission`, `saint/St.`, `temple`, `grace`, `mercy`, `healing`, `fellowship`, `Salvation Army`, historically religious universities/hospitals.
- **Negative / exclusion**: `without regard to religion`, anti-discrimination lists, purely geographic saint names, medical cross, corporate “mission statement,” “ministry” as government department outside U.S., “temple” as anatomy/cultural venue, “church street” addresses.
- **Administrative priors**: NTEE `X`, IRS `Religious Purpose`, Schedule A church/convention status; use as priors, not ground truth, because many faith-inspired hospitals/schools/social services sit outside NTEE X.

### Human annotation instructions

1. Code **identity** and **activity** separately.
2. Mark **historical roots only** when religion appears only in founding history and not current mission/activity.
3. Require an evidence span for every positive label.
4. Assign **ambiguous/review** when only weak terms appear (`mission`, `ministry`, `faith`, `spiritual`) without named tradition or practice.
5. Do not assume Christianity: include Jewish, Islamic, Hindu, Buddhist, Sikh, Indigenous, interfaith, and nonreligious categories.
6. For denomination, prefer official affiliation over generic self-description; if only local congregation is given, search/ask for denominational affiliation.

## 6. Gaps, Caveats, and Read-First List

**Major caveats**

- **Christian/English bias**: Most U.S. nonprofit typologies and dictionaries over-detect Protestant/Christian visible markers and under-detect Jewish, Catholic, Muslim, Hindu, Buddhist, Indigenous, and implicit/practical-theology expressions.
- **Ambiguous terms**: `mission`, `ministry`, `service`, `grace`, `saint`, `temple`, `spiritual`, and `faith` need context.
- **Identity vs practice vs belief**: Survey and organizational sources consistently warn these are different constructs.
- **Mode and question-order effects**: Pew, GSS reports, and recent Survey Practice work show religion measures change with mode, response order, “if any,” and one-step vs two-step designs.
- **Cross-national validity**: WVS/EVS/ISSP comparisons show “religion/affiliation/belonging” may not translate cleanly, especially in East Asian and multi-religious contexts.
- **Administrative labels are incomplete**: NTEE X and IRS religious-purpose flags miss many religiously affiliated schools, hospitals, charities, and international NGOs.

**Read first**

1. Sider & Unruh (2004) for the organization/program typology.
2. Pew (2018, 2021) for reusable affiliation and evangelical/denomination wording.
3. NCS cumulative codebook for congregational affiliation and denomination coding.
4. Bielefeld & Cleveland (2013) for definitional and methodological pitfalls.
5. Ebaugh/Chafetz/Pipes (2003/2006) for visible religiosity indicators.
6. ARDA Measurement Wizard and WVS/EVS for question wording variants.
7. Ma (2021) and Fyall/Gugerty/Moore (2018) for mission-text classification.
