## Synthesis: Measurement Issues for Religious Mission Classification

1. **Religious mission is multidimensional**: literature separates religious identity, affiliation, governance, funding, symbols, staff/board selection, service content, proselytizing, and target beneficiaries. A binary label is useful but should be documented as “observable religious mission/expression in short text,” not true religiosity.
2. **NTEE X is high-value but incomplete**: it is an official administrative label, but NTEE is usually single-primary-purpose, sometimes missing/incorrect, and poor at multi-mission organizations.
3. **Churches/congregations are undercovered in Form 990**: IRS says churches and some church-affiliated organizations are excepted from annual filing. Use BMF/NCS/ARDA/directories for congregational benchmarks.
4. **Short text creates false positives/negatives**: names like “St. Mary’s Hospital” may indicate religious origin but not current mission; secularized faith-founded organizations may omit religious language; non-Christian traditions may use less obvious or less Protestant-coded language.
5. **Organization vs program level matters**: an organization may be religious while a program description is secular, or a secular organization may run religious/chaplaincy programs.
6. **Taxonomies mix purpose, activity, and identity**: NTEE, IRS purpose codes, PCS, NAICS, ICNPO/ICNP-TSO, and UK-CAT answer different questions. For classification, define whether the label means primary sector, religious auspice, religious service content, or mission orientation.
7. **Class imbalance matters**: reported mission-classifier results show high accuracy can hide weak specificity/sensitivity. Use precision/recall/F1, calibration, and threshold audits, not accuracy alone.

## Practical Recommendations

### Labels

- Use a **multi-label schema** rather than only binary:
  - `religious_purpose_explicit` (mission/description says religious, worship, ministry, evangelism, faith formation, etc.)
  - `religious_identity_or_affiliation` (denomination, congregation, church/mosque/synagogue/temple, saint names, faith-founded identity)
  - `religious_service_content` (prayer, scripture, chaplaincy, evangelism, missionary activity)
  - `religion_related_NTEE_X` (administrative label)
  - `faith_inspired_ambiguous` (religious origin/branding but mission text not explicitly religious)
- Keep a derived binary only after specifying threshold rules, e.g. positive if explicit purpose OR NTEE X OR high-confidence religious identity.

### Validation Data and Benchmarks

- **Positive/negative administrative labels**: IRS/NCCS NTEE X vs non-X; IRS 1023-EZ religious purpose codes; old IRS activity codes for churches/missions/evangelism where available.
- **External benchmarks**: National Congregations Study, ARDA/ICPSR congregation data, denominational directories, Candid PCS auspice/subject, Cause IQ multi-code/manual updates.
- **Human-coded audit sample**: stratify by predicted probability, NTEE group, organization type (congregation, school, hospital, human service, international NGO, foundation), and religion-coded name tokens.
- **Gold labels**: code visible mission text plus website/990 purpose statement for a smaller sample; separately record “insufficient information.”

### Audit Samples

- Oversample likely errors: `St./Saint`, `YMCA/YWCA`, hospitals/universities with religious founders, Jewish/Muslim/Sikh/Buddhist/Hindu organizations, international relief NGOs, foundations funding religious entities, and organizations with generic “mission/ministry/service” terms.
- Report class-specific metrics for religious positives: precision, recall, specificity, F1, calibration, and subgroup performance by tradition and organization type.

## Gaps, Caveats, and Read-First List

**Read first**: Ma (2021), Santamarina et al. (2023), Fyall et al. (2018), Sider & Unruh (2004), Bielefeld & Cleveland (2013), NCCS NTEE docs, IRS church filing rules, Nonprofit Mission Classifiers docs.

**Gaps/caveats**:

- Few peer-reviewed papers classify specifically “religious mission” from short nonprofit text at scale; most classify NTEE/ICNPO sectors or study FBO definitions qualitatively.
- Proprietary sources (Candid/GuideStar, Cause IQ) may be excellent validation data but access/licensing can constrain reproducibility.
- Religious construct labels risk Christian/English-language bias if built from obvious tokens only.
- Form 990 e-file text is rich for filing nonprofits but misses many churches and small/unregistered religious groups.
