## Why These Papers Matter

- **LLM-as-annotator validity**: Gilardi et al. show the upside; Pangakis et al., Ziems et al., and 2025-2026 prompt-sensitivity work show why validation, prompt perturbation, and human audits remain essential.
- **Social science measurement**: Heseltine & Clemm von Hohenberg, Törnberg, Ornstein et al., and Mellon et al. are closest to applied political/CSS text-as-data workflows where labels become research variables or training data.
- **Weak supervision**: Ratner et al. and Smith et al. give the correct architecture for combining rules, LLM prompts, distant labels, and abstentions instead of treating one source as ground truth.
- **Noisy-label training**: Zhu et al., SaFER, LAFT, Agro & Aldarmaki, Lu & Smith, and Ye et al. show that realistic/feature-dependent/LLM noise differs from random flips and needs audit sets, robust training, and calibration/disagreement-aware filtering.

## Synthesis for Training a Binary Mission-Text Classifier

1. Treat LLM and rule labels as **noisy measurements**, not truth. Mission text likely has boundary ambiguity: organizations may state mixed social, economic, religious, or public-service objectives.
2. Use **multiple label sources**: keyword/rule LFs, embedding-neighbor heuristics, LLM prompts with different operationalizations, and possibly negative-label rules. Aggregate with a probabilistic label model or at least compare against majority vote.
3. Build a **small expert audit set** before large-scale labeling. Literature repeatedly finds high aggregate accuracy can still produce biased downstream estimates or divergent decision boundaries.
4. Binary classification helps, but false-positive/false-negative costs differ. LLM-trained classifiers may over-predict the positive class or encode a broader concept boundary than humans.
5. Fine-tuning BERT/RoBERTa on weak labels can work, but evaluate against gold labels and monitor seed instability, calibration, minority subgroup performance, and early plateaus.

## Practical Recommendations for Experiments and Validation

1. **Annotation design**: draft a codebook; label 300-800 stratified examples by humans; include borderline, short, long, and sector-diverse mission statements.
2. **Prompt robustness**: run 3-5 semantically equivalent prompts and at least two model families if feasible; log disagreement as uncertainty.
3. **Weak supervision setup**: implement LFs with abstain behavior; record LF coverage, conflict, empirical precision on audit set, and pairwise correlation.
4. **Label aggregation**: compare majority vote, Snorkel label model, LLM-only, rules-only, and hybrid LLM+rules. Train end models on both hard and probabilistic soft labels.
5. **Filtering/sampling**: prioritize human review for high LF/LLM disagreement, high LLM entropy, prompt flips, high model-loss examples, and strata where audit precision is low.
6. **Fine-tuning**: baseline RoBERTa/BERT with early stopping; add label smoothing, confidence-weighted loss, soft-label training, and seed ensembles; consider SaFER/LAFT-style confidence guidance if labels are highly noisy.
7. **Evaluation**: report F1, precision/recall, PR-AUC, calibration/ECE or Brier score, seed variance, threshold curves, and subgroup/sector errors; choose threshold by application cost, not default 0.5.
8. **Human-in-the-loop loop**: after first model, audit false positives/negatives and high-confidence disagreements; revise codebook/LFs; retrain; freeze final audit set for unbiased evaluation.

## Gaps, Caveats, and Read-First Papers

- **Read first**: Gilardi et al. (2023), Pangakis et al. (2023), Ziems et al. (2024), Ratner et al. (2017), Smith et al. (2024), Zhu et al. (2022), Wang et al. (2023), Lu & Smith (2025).
- **Caveats**: LLM confidence is not always calibrated; prompt choice can shift measurement; human labels are also noisy; replacing human labels entirely is weakest for ambiguous constructs; arXiv 2025-2026 findings need replication.
- **Research gap**: No central benchmark for organization mission text; opportunity to contribute a labeled/audited mission-text dataset and compare LLM-only, weak-supervision, and transformer fine-tuning pipelines.
